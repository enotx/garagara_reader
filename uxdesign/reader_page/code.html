The morning light was pale, casting long shadows across the stone floor. Outside, the great wind of late autumn had finally quieted into a shivering rustle among the withered hedgerows.

For several silent minutes, she sat immovable at the oak bureau, tapping her quill against the unblemished vellum. She took his advice into account before she wrote the letter. The ink remained still wet, mirroring the dim rectangular sky framed by the mullioned window.

There was no mistaking what had to be said next, yet every phrase felt laden with irrevocable consequence. He would read it by candlelight at dusk, miles away across the misted valley, unsuspecting and alone.

She leaned back against the high spindle chair and breathed slowly, letting the gravity of her resolution take root.
